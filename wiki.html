Start here

Watch Later Enhanced is a private watch list for YouTube. You save videos with one click while you browse, and they wait for you in the extension popup — no playlists, no clutter, no account.

1

Save

Hold Alt and left click any video on YouTube. A short confirmation appears on screen.

2

Open the popup

Click the extension icon in the browser toolbar to see everything you saved.

3

Watch

Click a title to open the video, or send it to the floating mini player.

Can't find the extension icon? Click the puzzle-piece button in the browser toolbar and pin Watch Later Enhanced so it stays visible.

Saving a video

There are four ways to add a video to your list. All of them save the same thing.

1. Alt + left click on a thumbnail

Anywhere on YouTube — home page, search results, a channel, the sidebar of recommendations — hold Alt and left click a video's thumbnail or title. It is the ordinary click that saves; right and middle click are left to YouTube and to your browser. The video is saved and YouTube does not navigate away, so you can keep browsing and keep saving.

2. Alt + left click on the player

While you are watching something, hold Alt and left click the video player itself to save the video you are on.

3. The button under the video

On a video page, Watch Later Enhanced adds a Save to Watch Later Enhanced button next to YouTube's own Like and Share buttons.

4. Paste its link in the popup

For a link somebody sent you, or a video you are not looking at: copy the link, open the popup and paste it (Ctrl + V, or ⌘ + V on a Mac) anywhere on the list. A bar at the bottom confirms it.

There is also a box for it: click the gear icon and open Add & back up, paste the link into Add by link and press Enter, or click Add. The box is the one to use when you want to be told why a link was refused — a paste onto the list that is not a YouTube video link is simply ignored.

  • Any YouTube link to a video or a Short works — from the address bar, from the Share button (youtu.be/…), from the mobile site — with or without https:// in front. The full list is under Links it understands.
  • Anything else is refused, with a line saying why: a link to another website, or a YouTube link that is not a video, such as a playlist or a channel.
  • The video goes to the end of To Watch. If it is already on your list, you are told so — and in which tab — instead of getting it twice.
Why the title arrives a moment later. The popup cannot ask YouTube anything — that is part of what keeps it private — so a video added by its link shows its link, in grey, with a Title pending label, until a YouTube tab has looked it up. That happens at once if YouTube is open on screen, and otherwise the next time you open it. The channel and whether it is a Short are filled in the same way. A YouTube tab that was open before the extension last updated cannot do it until it is reloaded.

What you get back

  • A dark confirmation panel appears in the middle of the page with the video title.
  • A short sound plays (you can turn this off in Settings).
  • If the video is already on your list, the panel says Already saved! instead of adding a duplicate.
Shorts work too. Regular videos and Shorts are both saved, and both end up as a normal watch link in your list — so a Short opens as an ordinary video when you come back to it. Its title is read from the Shorts player itself, because on those pages the browser tab is called just "YouTube".

Your list

The popup has three tabs. The number on each tab tells you how many videos are inside.

To Watch

Everything you saved and haven't watched yet. This is the tab you will use most.

Archive

Videos you marked as watched. They leave To Watch but are not deleted.

Trash

Removed videos, kept for a while in case you change your mind.

Favourites

Every row has a heart. Click it and it fills in red; click it again and it empties. The heart beside the All / Videos / Shorts chips shows only your favourites, and carries a count of how many that would be.

Favourites are not a fourth kind of video — they narrow whatever you are already looking at. Pick Shorts and press the heart and you get your favourite Shorts; type a tag as well and you get the favourites carrying that tag.

What each row tells you

Every card carries its number in the list, in the corner on its left, counting from 1. Hover the card and the number becomes the grip you drag it by — they are the same thing said two ways, so they share the spot and nothing moves when they swap. While a filter or a search is on you keep seeing each video's real number, so a result numbered 23 is the twenty-third in your queue, not the third thing that matched.

Under every title sits a small label — Video or Shorts — and next to it the channel that published it. A long channel name is shortened; hover it to read the whole thing. Your own tags go on their own line underneath, so the two never crowd each other.

Under the tabs, three chips narrow the list to All, Videos or Shorts, each with a count of how many there are. The choice sticks until you change it, and works alongside the favourites toggle and the tag search that share the line with them.

The type is worked out from the page a video was saved from, and then confirmed with YouTube in the background — because the same Short can be opened either as a Short or as an ordinary video, and from a /watch link nothing on the page says which it is. A label that starts out wrong corrects itself a moment later.

Items you saved before a detail existed — a type, a channel — are not left blank, and neither are videos added by their link or imported without a title. A few of them are filled in each time you open a YouTube page, quietly in the background, the ones missing a title first. A long list takes a while to work through, and needs nothing from you.

Undo

After you archive or remove a video, a small bar appears at the bottom of the popup with an Undo link for a couple of seconds. It puts the video back exactly where it was.

How the Trash behaves

  • The Trash keeps your 50 most recent removals. Older ones drop off on their own to keep storage small.
  • Empty trash, at the top of the Trash tab, clears it in one go.
  • Emptying the Trash and deleting permanently cannot be undone.

Tags & search

Tags are free-form labels — cooking, work, watch tonight, anything you like. A video can have as many as you want.

Adding a tag

  1. Click the tag button on the video's row.
  2. Type a name (up to 22 characters). Tags you already use are suggested as you type.
  3. Press Enter to add it. Press Esc to cancel.

Each tag gets its own colour automatically, derived from its name — the same word always gets the same colour. Settings → Styling decides whether tags are coloured that way, left grey, or all given one colour you choose.

Finding videos again

  • Type what you remember. Click the magnifying glass at the end of the filter row — or press / — and a search box opens underneath. What you type is matched against the title of every saved video and against your tags; partial words are fine.
  • Click a channel name, under any video, to see only what you have saved from that channel. It stays lit while the filter holds, in a colour the channel's own name decides — the same way a tag gets its colour. Click the lit one again to take the filter off.
  • Click a tag to see only the videos carrying it.
  • Press Esc, or click the magnifying glass again, to clear the search and fold the box away.

Clicking a channel or a tag writes what it did into the search box — channel:Fireship, tag:music. You can type those yourself, and edit or delete them like any other search. They match the whole name rather than part of it, so channel:Lofi does not also bring in Lofi Girl.

The box is folded away when nothing is being searched for, which leaves room for about half a video more in the list. It never folds away while it is filtering: if a search is active — because you typed one, or because you clicked a tag — the box stays open with the text in it, so the list can never be quietly filtered by something you cannot see.

Removing a tag

Click the small × inside the tag itself. It only affects that one video.

Tags are for what a video is to you — its title is already searchable without them. Tag the things you will want to find by a word that is not in the title.

Reordering your list

In the To Watch tab, grab the handle on the left of a row and drag it up or down to set your own order. Drag near the top or bottom edge and the list scrolls by itself.

Dragging is switched off while a tag search is active — otherwise the positions you see would not match the real list. Clear the search and the handles come back.

Detached mini player

The mini player pops the video out of the browser into a small floating window that stays on top of your other applications. You can drag it anywhere on screen, resize it, and keep watching while the browser is minimized or you work in another program.

Six ways to open it

A video you have not opened — anywhere its thumbnail appears: the home page, a search, a channel, the sidebar beside another video.

  • Alt + Shift + left click it. The same gesture that saves a video, with Shift, plays it in the mini player instead — on the picture or on the title, either one. This is the one that asks nothing of YouTube's own page furniture, so it is the one to reach for if anything below is having a bad day after a YouTube redesign.
  • Open the ⋮ menu on the video — the same one that holds "Save to Watch Later" — and pick Play in mini player at the top.
  • Hover the thumbnail: a button appears in its top-left corner, opposite YouTube's own Watch later and queue buttons. It stays put while YouTube's preview starts playing over the thumbnail, and follows the thumbnail while you scroll.

The video you are watching, or one already on your list:

  • The mini player button in YouTube's own control bar, at the bottom right of the video. Careful not to confuse it with YouTube's own miniplayer button a few icons away, which only shrinks the video inside the tab: ours is two windows with a play symbol, YouTube's is a single screen with a small block in its corner.
  • Alt + Shift + left click the player itself. Same gesture as everywhere else: on a video you have open it hands the live player over, so your position, the captions and the quality you picked all come with it. Doing it again puts the video back.
  • The same two-window button on any row in the extension popup. The video starts playing right away in a small window of its own — no tab, nothing to confirm.

They are all the same feature, so they all carry the same icon: a player floating in front of a window, with a play symbol in it.

Pinning it on top

The mini player is a window of its own, however you opened it. It survives minimizing the browser and closing the page it came from, but other windows can cover it.

To put it above everything, click Pin on top in its corner. The video moves into a floating player that stays on top of every other application, and the window it came from minimizes itself so only one window is left on screen. Close the pinned player and that window comes back with the video in it.

That window is minimized rather than closed because it has to stay: a floating player belongs to the window that opened it, and closing that window closes the player with it. It is the same rule that decides everything above — which is why opening the mini player from a video page gives you a window rather than a floating player. One tied to the tab would die the moment you closed it.

Pinning is a button rather than a setting on purpose: browsers only hand out an always-on-top window in answer to a click on the page itself, so nothing can ask for one on your behalf.

While it is open

  • YouTube's own controls are there, so play, volume, captions, quality, chapters and the skip-ad button all work as usual.
  • The page you opened it from stops playing, so you never end up with two soundtracks — one of them from a window you cannot see.
  • There is only ever one mini player. Asking for the video that is already in it brings that window forward; asking for another puts it in the same window.
  • The window remembers the size you gave it and opens that way next time.
  • The browser draws that window with its own small title bar, and that bar already carries the buttons to close it and to go back to the tab. Watch Later Enhanced does not draw its own copies underneath: over the video you get the title and a Pinned badge, and both fade away while you watch.

That title bar belongs to the browser, not to the page, so no extension can hide or restyle it — the video simply starts below it.

Closing it

Close the mini player window the way you close any window — its own ×, or Esc. Nothing else has to be tidied up: the page you opened it from kept its player, it just stopped playing it.

A pinned player is the one exception, because it is a floating player rather than a window: closing it hands the video back to the mini player window it came from, which comes out of the taskbar with it. The buttons for that are the ones your browser draws on its own small title bar — the × and the one that goes back — so the extension does not repeat them. What it draws over the video is the title and the Pinned badge, and both fade away while you watch.

What your browser needs

  • Chrome, Edge and other Chromium browsers, version 116 or newer: the full floating window described above.
  • Browsers without it: the extension falls back to the classic picture-in-picture window, which also floats on top but carries only basic controls.
  • Neither available: a short message appears on the page and nothing else changes.
You can switch the whole feature off under Settings → Detached mini player. That hides the button in YouTube's control bar, the button in the popup and disables the shortcut.

Settings

Open the popup and click the gear icon in the top-right corner. Everything you can change sits under Settings, folded away until you click it. Its closed header names anything you have switched off or changed, so closing it hides the controls and not what they are set to. Above it sit the things that are not settings: this guide, a link to the list of releases on GitHub if you want to know what each version added, and Add & back up, folded the same way, which holds what you can do to the list as a whole.

Add & back up

Add by link saves a video from its address — see Saving a video. Export and Import save your list to a file and read one back in; the Guide button beside them opens Import & export, which explains both step by step, with examples.

User guide

The book button that opened this page. It works offline — the guide ships inside the extension.

What's new

Next to the guide, a link to the releases on GitHub: the full list of what each version changed. The version you are running is printed at the bottom of the Info & Settings panel.

Sound

All sounds is the master switch: off means silence, wherever the sound would have come from.

Below it is a mixer with one row per sound. Click is what you hear in the popup; Save is what plays on YouTube when a video is added. Each has its own switch and its own level — drag the slider, or type a number from 0 to 100 in the box beside it. Muting a sound keeps the level you gave it, so switching it back on sounds the way it did.

Detached mini player

Turns the floating mini player on and off, everywhere. It is on by default.

Styling

Tags and the highlighted channel pill pick their own colours. This is where you decide how:

  • Random — the default. Every name gets its own colour, worked out from the letters in it, so the same tag is always the same colour and two people writing the same tag see the same thing.
  • Muted — no colour at all. One neutral grey for everything, like the rest of the popup.
  • Static — one colour everywhere, the one you give it. Type a HEX code or use the swatch to pick one. If the colour is a light one, the lettering flips to dark so the tag stays readable.

Glass turns the popup into frosted panels over a lit backdrop. Drag it up and the cards, the header and the settings panel go translucent and blur whatever is behind them — the list behind the settings panel, the backdrop behind everything else — while that backdrop picks up a soft wash of colour. At 0 the popup is the flat panel it has always been, and none of the blurring is drawn at all.

The popup's height is not a setting. It takes about three quarters of whatever height your screen has spare, between 400 and the 600 pixels a browser will draw — more videos before you scroll on a desktop, nothing overflowing on a small laptop.
Not the page behind the popup. A browser popup is a window of its own, drawn on a base the browser paints, and nothing inside it can reach past that — so the glass is made from the popup's own layers, not from the YouTube page underneath.

JobsMatch banner

The extension is free. A small note about JobsMatch sits under the header to support the project. The switch that hides it is locked until you are a supporter — press it and it says how.

Supporter code

After a tip on Ko-fi you receive a code. Paste it in the box at the foot of the Settings section and press Unlock; the JobsMatch switch above it is then yours, and the box disappears. Nothing is sent anywhere to check the code — it is verified on your own machine.

Import & export

Export saves your whole list into one file on your computer. Import reads a file like that back in. Together they are how you keep a backup, move your list to another browser or computer, or add a batch of videos in one go.

Both are in the popup: click the gear icon, then open Add & back up. The Guide button on the same line opens this page.

Export your list

  1. Open the popup, click the gear icon, and open Add & back up.
  2. Click Export.
  3. A file named like watch-later-enhanced-2026-09-28.json — with today's date — is saved wherever your browser saves downloads, usually the Downloads folder. The line under the button says how many videos it holds.

The file holds every video in To Watch and Archive, in your order, with its title, channel, type, tags, heart and dates. The Trash is not included, and neither are your settings — only the list.

Import a file

  1. Open the popup, click the gear icon, and open Add & back up.
  2. Click Import. A small window opens with your list and the Import button waiting.
  3. Click Import again in that window and choose the file.
  4. The line under the buttons says what happened, for example Added 12 videos · 3 already on your list. The new videos are in your list straight away.
Why a window first? Browsers close the toolbar popup as soon as something else takes the focus — Firefox and Chrome on Linux do it for the window you choose a file in — and the import would close with it, without a word. The small window cannot be closed that way, so the file is always chosen from there. Close it when you are done.

What importing does — and never does

  • It only adds. New videos go to the end of your list, in the order the file has them: to To Watch, or to Archive if the file says they were watched.
  • It never overwrites. A video already on your list is left exactly as it is — its title, tags, heart and place stay yours, whatever the file says about it.
  • It never removes. Nothing on your list disappears, so importing the same file twice is harmless: the second time says Nothing new to add.
  • It skips what is not a YouTube video — a link to another website, a playlist, a channel — and says how many it skipped. The rest are imported.
  • It can be undone. For a few seconds afterwards, the bar at the bottom of the popup offers Undo, which takes out exactly the videos that import added and nothing else.

Moving your list to another computer or browser

  1. On the old one, click Export.
  2. Get the file to the new one: a USB stick, an email to yourself, a cloud folder.
  3. Install Watch Later Enhanced there, then Import the file.

Chrome, Edge and Firefox all read the same file, so it moves between browsers too.

Writing a file yourself

You do not need every field, or an export to start from. The smallest file Import accepts is a list of links, one per line, each in quotes, with a comma after every one but the last: 

[
  "https://youtu.be/dQw4w9WgXcQ",
  "https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jNQXAC9IVRw",
  "youtube.com/watch?v=9bZkp7q19f0"
]

Each one arrives the way a video added by its link does: showing its link until a YouTube tab fills in the title, the channel and the type.

To give some videos more than a link, write them as entries with only the fields you want:

{
  "videos": [
    {
      "url": "https://youtu.be/dQw4w9WgXcQ",
      "tags": ["music", "80s"],
      "favourite": true
    },
    {
      "url": "https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9bZkp7q19f0",
      "title": "Gangnam Style",
      "watched": true,
      "watchedAt": "2026-09-01"
    }
  ]
}

The first goes to To Watch with two tags and a heart, and gets its title from YouTube. The second goes straight to Archive with the title you gave it. Links and entries can be mixed in one list.

Every field

FieldWhat it holdsIf it is missing
url The video's link — any of the forms below. The only field that is required. The entry is skipped.
title The title to show, as text. Filled in from YouTube.
channel Who published it, as text. Filled in from YouTube.
kind "video" or "short". A /shorts/ link is a Short whatever this says. Found out from YouTube.
tags A list of names, ["music", "80s"]. A tag longer than 22 characters is shortened; the same tag twice is kept once. No tags.
favourite true for a heart. favorite works too. No heart.
watched true puts the video in Archive. To Watch.
savedAt When it was saved: a date such as "2026-09-28" or "2026-09-28T18:30:00Z", or a number of milliseconds since 1970, as the extension keeps them. The moment of the import.
watchedAt When it was marked watched, written the same way. Left empty.

True and false, and numbers, are written without quotes; everything else goes in straight double quotes. Anything else in the file — other fields, colours, notes you left for yourself — is ignored. The file can be a list on its own, like the smallest example, or an object with the list under "videos", the way Export writes it.

Links it understands

The same links work in Add by link and in a file, with or without https:// in front. Anything after the video's id — a start time like &t=42s, a ?si=… from the Share button, a playlist — is fine and is dropped.

If something goes wrong

The line saysWhat it means, and what to do
That file is not valid JSON (line 4) The text breaks JSON's rules on or just before that line. It is nearly always a missing comma between two entries, a comma after the last one, or curly quotes (“ ”) where straight ones (" ") belong. Fix that line in a text editor and import again.
There is no list of videos in that file The file is JSON, but it has no list in it. Put the entries between [ and ], or under "videos".
The list in that file is empty The list is there, with nothing in it. Nothing was changed.
None of the … entries in that file is a YouTube video link Every entry lacks a url, or has one that is misspelled or points somewhere else. Compare them with the table of links above.
… skipped, not a YouTube video link Everything else was imported; only those entries were left out. Correct them and import the file again — what is already on your list will not be doubled.
That file is … MB, far bigger than any list The file chosen is over 20 MB, which is not a list — even ten thousand videos come to a fraction of that. Choose the file Export saved.
Nothing was imported: the browser would not save the list The browser refused the write. When the bar at the bottom of the popup adds storage may be full, empty the Trash and delete what you no longer need, then import again. Nothing on your list was changed.

Tips for editing a file by hand

  • Use a plain text editor — Notepad, TextEdit in plain-text mode, VS Code. A word processor quietly turns straight quotes into curly ones, which JSON does not accept.
  • Every entry in a list ends with a comma, except the last.
  • Keep the .json ending when you save.
  • Unsure? Export first, and edit a copy of what it made — it is already in the right shape.
The file never leaves your computer. Export writes it from inside your browser and Import reads it the same way: nothing is uploaded, to the developer or anyone else. Keep it somewhere safe — it is your list.

Privacy & your data

  • Your list lives in your own browser, in the extension's local storage. There is no account, no sign-in and no server holding your data.
  • Nothing you save is sent anywhere. The extension makes two kinds of request, both to YouTube and both only from a YouTube tab: one to read a video's title and channel when the page does not give them — or when the video was added by its link — and one to tell a Short from an ordinary video. Each carries the video and nothing about you. The popup itself makes none, which is why a video added there by its link waits for a YouTube tab to name it.
  • The extension only runs on YouTube pages. It cannot see any other site you visit.
  • Because the list is stored in this browser profile, it does not follow you to another computer on its own — and removing the extension removes the list with it.
  • Export and Import work on a file on your own computer. Nothing is uploaded.
Moving to a new machine, or removing the extension? Export your list first, and import the file wherever you want it back — Import & export shows how.

Troubleshooting

A setting I changed has no effect on YouTube

The popup always runs the newest version of the extension, but a YouTube tab that was already open keeps running the version it was loaded with. Right after an update, that tab can still be on the old one — so a sound level, or anything else that takes effect on the page, looks like it is being ignored.

Reload the YouTube tab (F5) and try again. If you installed the extension yourself from source, reload it first at chrome://extensions, then reload the tab. Once the tab is running the current version, settings apply straight away with no further reloading.

Alt + left click opened the video instead of saving it

The click has to be a left click, and it has to land on the video's thumbnail or its title. Hold Alt first, then click, and keep the key down until the confirmation appears. On some Linux desktops Alt + drag is reserved by the system for moving windows — a plain click still works.

The Save button isn't under the video

YouTube sometimes finishes drawing its buttons late. Reload the page. If the button is still missing, the video page may be an unusual layout — Alt + left click on the player always works.

The mini player button isn't in the control bar

  • Check Settings → Detached mini player is on.
  • Reload the video page.
  • Very old browsers have no support for floating windows at all — see the requirements above.

No button appears when I hover a thumbnail

First: reload the YouTube tab. A tab that was already open when the extension updated keeps running the old version until it is reloaded, and that alone accounts for most of it.

If it still does not appear, YouTube has most likely changed how it builds that part of the page. The hover button is the only way in that has to find its way around YouTube's own markup — so use Alt + Shift + left click, or Play in mini player in the video's ⋮ menu, both of which work the same on every page. A bug report with the page you were on is welcome; it is how that button gets fixed for everyone.

The mini player button in the popup opened a tab instead of a window

The button asks the extension's background worker for a window, and falls back to an ordinary tab only when it cannot reach it — which happens on browsers that do not let an extension open a window of its own. Reload the extension at chrome://extensions and try again; if it keeps opening a tab, use the mini player button in the player's control bar, or Alt + Shift + left click on the player, both of which open the window from the page itself.

I got a small player inside the page instead of a floating window

That is YouTube's own miniplayer, which lives in the corner of the tab and disappears when you minimize the browser. Its button sits in the same control bar as ours, and the two look deliberately different: YouTube's is a single screen with a small block in its corner, and the Watch Later Enhanced one is two windows with a play symbol.

The mini player closed by itself

The window itself outlives the page it came from, so navigating or closing that tab does not touch it. A pinned player is the exception: it is a floating player rather than a window, and it belongs to the window that opened it — closing that window closes the player with it, handing the video back. Unpin it, or leave its window minimized where the pin put it.

"Could not save — storage may be full"

The browser refused the write because the extension's storage is full. Empty the Trash and permanently delete what you no longer need.

A video I added by its link still shows the link

Its title comes from a YouTube tab, since the popup cannot ask YouTube anything. Open YouTube, or switch to a YouTube tab you already have, and it fills in within a few seconds. A YouTube tab that was open before the extension last updated is still running the old version — reload it (F5) first.

A video I added by its link says "Unknown Title"

YouTube was asked and said it could not describe that video: it is private or has been deleted, or the link was mistyped. Click the title to see what YouTube says. If the video does play, save it once more from its own page with Alt + left click — that replaces Unknown Title with the real one. If it does not, remove it and add the correct link.

A video shows the wrong title

Titles are read from YouTube when you save. If the request fails, the extension falls back to the text next to the thumbnail, which is occasionally truncated. Remove the video and save it again from the video's own page for the exact title.
